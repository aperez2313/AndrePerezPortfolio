## 2. Message hierarchy

What a visitor learns, in order:

1. **What Andre fixes.** Processes that have outgrown their tools. Source: the positioning statement.
2. **How.** Find the friction, build the fix, document it so it keeps working after handoff. Source: the mission.
3. **Proof.** Case studies with a problem, his part, and evidence. Then the resume.
4. **Who.** Bilingual English and Spanish, Miami, BBA in MIS, the four values shown through work.

The 30-second action: open a case study. Second: download the resume. Third: email or LinkedIn.

## 3. Website tone for Home and About

The kit defines resume, LinkedIn, and email tone but not website tone. Proposed:

- First person, present tense, short sentences. "I" for Andre's work, "we" for the team.
- Lead with the situation the reader recognizes, then what Andre did about it.
- No adjectives about Andre. The work carries the description.
- Plain enough that a store manager and a CTO both get it on the first read.
- Closer to a conversation than a resume. Never a template.

Three sample lines, true to the resume as it stands:

1. "Two supermarket banners, three delivery partners, one storefront, and a catalog that has to match on all of them. That is the job."
2. "When the same problem comes back a third time, I stop fixing it by hand and build the check."
3. "I write the steps down so the store can run it without calling me."

2. Status colors: no form at launch. Later, errors are words plus Night on Lilac, success is Night on Citron.

## 6. Content rules carried into the build

- Employers, delivery partners, Freshop/NCR Voyix, and tools are named in text. No logos. No fees, volumes, or partner terms.
- Phone number never shown. SKU count allowed for now.
- Job titles match the resume exactly.
- Every page and project passes the four checks: true and showable, mine to share, labeled work or learning, fine for my manager to read.
